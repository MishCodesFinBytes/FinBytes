“What’s your P&L?” sounds like one number. It isn’t. A trading book has half a dozen legitimate P&L figures that answer different questions, and confusing them is how desks, risk, finance, and product control end up arguing about the “same” number that never reconciles. This post walks through the main variations — what each one includes, why it exists, and how they fit together — then decomposes one option position every way at once.

1. What “P&L” actually measures

At heart, profit and loss is the change in the value of what you hold plus any cash that moved:

P&Ltoday = (Portfolio valuetoday − Portfolio valueyesterday) + Cash flowstoday

Every variation below is just a different rule for which value changes and cash flows you count. The two axes that generate most of the vocabulary are:

  • Has the position been closed? — realized vs unrealized.
  • What caused the change? — market moves only (clean) vs everything, including new trades and fees (dirty).

2. Realized vs unrealized P&L

TypeDefinitionExample
RealizedLocked in by closing a position — the cash is real and can’t change.Bought at 100, sold at 105 → +5 realized, done.
Unrealized (mark-to-market)The paper gain/loss on positions still open, valued at today’s market price.Bought at 100, still holding, marked at 103 → +3 unrealized, will move tomorrow.

Total P&L = realized + unrealized. The split matters because unrealized P&L is an estimate — it depends entirely on your marks. A book can show a large unrealized profit that evaporates the moment you try to sell into a thin market. That gap between mark and achievable price is exactly what reserves (bid-offer, liquidity, model reserves) are meant to cover.

3. The daily P&L identity: clean vs dirty

Overnight, three different things change a book’s value. Product control decomposes the total (“dirty”) P&L into them:

Dirty P&L = Clean (market) P&L + New-trade P&L + Fees, funding & adjustments

ComponentWhat it isAlso called
Clean P&LRevalue yesterday’s positions with today’s market data. No new trades, no fees. Isolates pure market movement.Market P&L, hypothetical P&L
New-trade P&LValue created (or destroyed) by trades executed today — mostly the bid-offer captured or crossed at execution.Day-1 P&L, DoD trade P&L
Fees / funding / adjustmentsCommissions, brokerage, funding cost of the position, reserve changes, valuation adjustments (XVA).—
Dirty P&LThe sum of all of it — the actual change in the book’s value. This is what hits the ledger.Actual P&L, total P&L

Trade-lifecycle events — amendments, cancels, novations, terminations, and maturities/exercises — are broken out separately for the same reason new trades are: a position stepping into or out of the book is not a market move. Letting any of them leak into clean P&L corrupts the very number risk is trying to predict (a classic source of unexplained P&L — see §7).

Why the distinction earns its keep: a desk can look flat on dirty P&L while its clean P&L was deeply negative, rescued only by aggressive new-trade margin. Risk cares about clean P&L, because that’s the number their risk model is supposed to predict. Finance cares about dirty P&L, because that’s what the firm actually earned.

4. P&L attribution: explaining the clean P&L

Clean P&L tells you the market moved you by, say, +$1.65. P&L attribution (“P&L explain,” PAA) tells you why — how much came from spot, from volatility, from the passage of time. For a derivatives book it is a Taylor expansion of the pricing function in its risk factors, i.e. the Greeks:

ΔV ≈ δ · ΔS + ½ · γ · ΔS² + ν · Δσ + Θ · Δt + ρ · Δr + … + unexplained

Attribution bucketGreekAnswers
Delta P&L (directional)δHow much did I make from the underlying moving?
Gamma P&L (convexity)γHow much extra because that move was large / my delta changed?
Vega P&LνHow much from implied volatility changing?
Theta / carry P&LΘHow much did I bleed (or earn) just from a day passing?
Rho P&LρHow much from the discount rate moving?
Unexplained—Everything the sensitivities didn’t capture (see §7).

The option Greeks above are just one asset class’s version of the expansion. Every desk runs the identical machinery on its own risk factors:

Volatilityν · Δσ  +  vanna (∂²V/∂S∂σ) · ΔS·Δσ  +  volga (∂²V/∂σ²) · Δσ²

Two details mark out someone who has actually run this: rates P&L is attributed on a bucketed key-rate basis, never a single parallel DV01 — a curve steepening has near-zero parallel move but plenty of P&L; and the vol bucket is more than vega — naming vanna and volga shows you know exactly where a first-order explain springs a leak.

5. Running example: one option, one day

Take the at-the-money call from the Greeks post and hold it overnight. Start-of-day inputs and the Black-Scholes Greeks:

Start of day (t0)Greeks at t0
S (spot)$100Delta δ0.6368
K (strike)$100Gamma γ0.0188
T (expiry)1.00 yrVega ν (per 1 vol pt)0.3752
r (rate)5%Theta Θ (per day)−0.0255
σ (vol)20%Price C0$10.4506

Overnight the market moves: spot 100 → 102 (ΔS = +2), implied vol 20% → 21% (Δσ = +1 pt), and one day passes (Δt = 1/252). Re-pricing the option with Black-Scholes at the new inputs gives:

C1 = $12.1034  →  Clean P&L (full revaluation) = 12.1034 − 10.4506 = +$1.6528

That is the “truth” — a full reprice. Now explain it with the Greeks.

6. Predicted P&L vs actual P&L

Two ways to get the clean P&L, and the desk computes both:

  • Actual (full revaluation) P&L — reprice the position from scratch at today’s market data. Exact, but a black box: it doesn’t tell you why.
  • Predicted (risk-based, Taylor) P&L — multiply yesterday’s Greeks by today’s market moves. Transparent and additive, but approximate.

Plugging the moves into the expansion:

BucketCalculationP&L
Delta P&L0.6368 × 2+1.2737
Gamma P&L½ × 0.0188 × 2²+0.0375
Vega P&L0.3752 × 1+0.3752

Predicted totalsum of the above+1.6610

The Greeks explain 99.5% of the move. A residual of −$0.008 on a $1.65 move is tiny — and that’s the point of a good attribution.

7. Unexplained P&L — where the residual comes from

Unexplained P&L is the gap between the full reprice and the sum of the risk-based buckets. It is the single most-scrutinised number in P&L attribution, because a large or persistent residual means your risk model doesn’t describe your book. Common sources:

  • Higher-order terms — the Taylor series was truncated, or the move was simply too large for a first/second-order expansion to hold. Third-order (speed) and beyond are dropped.
  • Cross-gamma / cross-effects — spot and vol (or rate and FX) moved together; the pure single-factor buckets miss the interaction.
  • Vanna and volga — a first-order vega alone can’t explain P&L when spot and vol both move (vanna, ∂²V/∂S∂σ) or vol moves a lot (volga, ∂²V/∂σ²).
  • Discontinuous payoffs — barriers, digitals, and callables near an exercise boundary, where the pricing function isn’t smooth and a Taylor expansion breaks down entirely.
  • Vol surface — a single vega assumes a parallel shift; real skew / term-structure moves need bucketed vega.
  • Unmapped risk factors — a basis, dividend, seasonality, or correlation the risk model doesn’t carry.
  • Stale / off-market marks & bad fixings — a wrong input, not a wrong model.
  • Snap-time mismatch — risk struck at the T−1 close but P&L at the T close, or intraday vs EOD; the two series see different market data.
  • Trade-population mis-tagging — a new trade leaking into market P&L (it belonged in the new-trade bucket), or an amend double-counted.
  • Model / library / config change — a pricer version bump or a parameter change between the two runs moves value with no market move at all.
  • Curve reconstruction differences — two systems bootstrap or interpolate the same curve slightly differently.
  • Missing cashflows, resets, or fixings — a coupon or reset not picked up on the day it happens.

A clean way to shrink the residual is a full-reval attribution: reprice moving one factor at a time (spot only, then +vol, then +time) so each bucket is itself a mini full-reval and the “unexplained” collapses to genuine cross-effects and truncation.

8. Risk explain — the sibling of P&L explain

Most candidates prepare P&L explain and stop there. The same discipline applies to risk: when your Greeks (or DV01s, vegas) change between two snapshots, you decompose that change the same way. Risk is state-dependent, so it drifts even with no trading — and a good desk explains why. Decompose Δrisk between two runs into:

BucketWhat moved the risk
Trade activityNew, amended, matured, or exercised trades adding or removing sensitivity.
Market movesRisk is state-dependent — gamma means delta moves on its own as spot moves; vanna means delta moves as vol moves.
Passage of timeTheta acting on the Greeks themselves — a day’s decay reshapes delta and gamma, not just value.
Model / config changeA pricer or parametrisation change that reshapes the sensitivities.
Static data changeA curve definition or instrument reference-data change.
ResidualTreated with the same discipline as unexplained P&L (§7) — investigated, not ignored.

The tell that you’ve actually done this: pointing out that gamma makes delta move without anyone trading. That one sentence separates a candidate who has run a risk explain from one who has only read about it.

9. Carry, theta, and funding — the P&L of doing nothing

Even with markets frozen, a book’s value drifts overnight. That drift is carry (a.k.a. the theta/time bucket for options):

  • Options — time decay. Our call loses −$0.0255/day of time value just from the clock. A long-option book bleeds theta; a short-option book earns it.
  • Bonds / cash — coupon accrual and “rolldown” the curve. You earn the coupon and, on an upward curve, the bond rolls to a lower yield and higher price.
  • Funding — the cost of financing the position (repo, unsecured funding). A leveraged long pays funding every day; that is real, negative carry.

Carry is the predictable, direction-free part of P&L. Isolating it lets a desk see whether it made money from taking a view (delta/vega) or simply from holding inventory (carry).

10. Regulatory view: FRTB P&L Attribution (PLA)

Under the Basel FRTB framework, a desk that wants to use its own internal model (IMA) instead of the punitive standardised approach must prove its risk model actually tracks its front-office P&L. The P&L Attribution (PLA) test compares two daily series over a trailing window (250 business days):

SeriesFull nameWho produces it / how
APLActual P&LFront office’s actual clean daily P&L (real prices, excludes fees & new deals).
HPLHypothetical P&LYesterday’s positions repriced on today’s market data with front-office models — positions frozen.
RTPLRisk-Theoretical P&LThe risk engine’s predicted P&L, using only the risk factors and sensitivities in the model.

PLA compares RTPL vs HPL: if the risk model’s factors are complete, its predicted P&L should track the hypothetical full reprice closely. Two statistics on the paired daily series decide the outcome:

  • Spearman rank correlation — do the two series move together (in rank)?
  • Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) statistic — do they have the same distribution?
ZoneCondition (BCBS d457)Consequence
GreenSpearman ≥ 0.80 and KS ≤ 0.09Desk keeps internal-model approval.
Amberbetween green and redAllowed, but a capital surcharge.
RedSpearman < 0.70 or KS > 0.12Desk falls back to standardised approach — far more capital.

This is the institutional reason unexplained P&L (§7) matters in hard money: a persistent residual pushes a desk from green to red and can multiply its capital charge. Good P&L attribution isn’t bookkeeping hygiene — it’s capital.

11. Economic vs accounting P&L

One last split, because it trips people up. The same trade can show two different P&Ls depending on the lens:

Economic (fair value / MTM)Accounting (accrual)
BasisToday’s market price of everything, every day.Recognise income over time per accounting rules (IFRS 9 / US GAAP).
VolatilityMarks move daily — noisy.Smoothed — a loan accrues interest steadily.
Day-1 P&LRecognise the bid-offer / model edge immediately.May defer day-1 P&L when inputs are unobservable (Level 3) and release it over the life of the trade.

Trading desks live in the economic/MTM world; banking-book and finance often live in the accrual world. When they disagree about “the P&L,” they’re usually both right — about different numbers.

12. Python: decompose the option P&L

The whole worked example in code — price and Greeks under Black-Scholes, then a first-order attribution against a full reprice:

from math import log, sqrt, exp, erf, pi

def _N(x):  return 0.5 * (1 + erf(x / sqrt(2)))
def _n(x):  return exp(-x * x / 2) / sqrt(2 * pi)

def call_and_greeks(S, K, T, r, sig):
    d1 = (log(S / K) + (r + sig * sig / 2) * T) / (sig * sqrt(T))
    d2 = d1 - sig * sqrt(T)
    price = S * _N(d1) - K * exp(-r * T) * _N(d2)
    delta = _N(d1)
    gamma = _n(d1) / (S * sig * sqrt(T))
    vega  = S * _n(d1) * sqrt(T)                      # per 1.00 (100%) vol
    theta = (-(S * _n(d1) * sig) / (2 * sqrt(T))
             - r * K * exp(-r * T) * _N(d2))          # per year
    return price, delta, gamma, vega, theta

# --- start of day ---
S0, K, T0, r, sig0 = 100, 100, 1.0, 0.05, 0.20
C0, delta, gamma, vega, theta = call_and_greeks(S0, K, T0, r, sig0)

# --- overnight market moves ---
dS, dsig, dt = +2.0, +0.01, 1 / 252
S1, sig1, T1 = S0 + dS, sig0 + dsig, T0 - dt
C1, *_ = call_and_greeks(S1, K, T1, r, sig1)

# --- attribution ---
pnl_full  = C1 - C0                      # actual (full revaluation)
pnl_delta = delta * dS
pnl_gamma = 0.5 * gamma * dS ** 2
pnl_vega  = vega * dsig                  # vega is per 1.00 vol; dsig in vol units
pnl_theta = theta * dt
predicted = pnl_delta + pnl_gamma + pnl_vega + pnl_theta
unexplained = pnl_full - predicted

print(f"delta      {pnl_delta:+.4f}")
print(f"gamma      {pnl_gamma:+.4f}")
print(f"vega       {pnl_vega:+.4f}")
print(f"theta      {pnl_theta:+.4f}")
print(f"predicted  {predicted:+.4f}")
print(f"full reval {pnl_full:+.4f}")
print(f"UNEXPLAINED{unexplained:+.4f}")

Same book, same day, eight legitimate answers. Knowing which one someone means — and which one you mean — is most of the skill.
